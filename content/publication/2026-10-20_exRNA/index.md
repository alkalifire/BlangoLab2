abstract: Extracellular RNAs (exRNAs) are emerging as potent bidirectional interkingdom effectors of host-pathogen interactions, particularly in plant and insect systems. Here, using small RNA-sequencing and genetic manipulation, we provide evidence consistent with a function of the human neutrophil exRNA-ome in modulating gene expression across kingdoms in the fungal pathogen Aspergillus fumigatus. The neutrophil extracellular space contains a diverse repertoire of RNA, which was corroborated using a droplet-digital stem-loop RT-PCR protocol to measure differential abundance of several tRNA-derived RNAs between two extracellular fractions. A closer inspection of the extracellular miRNA pool revealed enrichment of antimicrobial Let-7 family sRNAs and immunomodulatory miR-223, among others. Secretion of these sRNAs was largely impervious to infection status, suggesting that neutrophils produce and maintain an antimicrobial exRNA environment against A. fumigatus that requires further targeting. Cross-kingdom miRNA target prediction and exRNA delivery experiments revealed that putative fungal targets are modulated independently of the fungal argonaute proteins and in a donor-dependent manner. Although heterogeneous, for donors that exhibited a silencing effect, mutated target sequences in the fungus did reverse silencing. We hypothesize that the exRNA landscape produced by neutrophils contributes to their multifaceted role in antifungal defense.
